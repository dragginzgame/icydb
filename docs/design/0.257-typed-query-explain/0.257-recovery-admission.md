# Recovery-safe construction admission

Status: design proposal, 2026-09-10. The user approved developing this contract
before implementation. This refines R5 in the [remaining checklist](0.257-explain-remaining.md),
not a new feature, implemented guarantee or additional recovery stage.

Delivery update: the user authorized finishing diagnostic construction/rendering
and typed explain before the remaining shared preparation/recovery accounting.
This contract is still required for combined R5 and line closeout. Its proof
requirements are unchanged; no new recovery limit may land independently merely
because it is no longer a prerequisite for exposing typed diagnostics.

## Decision

Admit recovery construction before publishing a write. Use the existing row/key
preparation owner in the recovery contexts it already supports, with required
operation-wide construction budgets. Keep the existing mutation ceilings.

The conservative first implementation should perform recovery-shaped transition
preparation and final-after-image preparation before publication. These are
additional invocations of the maintained constructor, not a second encoder,
estimator, recovery simulator or apply route. Do not assume ordinary write
construction already pays for all future reconstruction.

The trade-off is real: writes do additional preparation and may reject smaller
batches than today. Avoid a configurable fast/unqualified mode. Reusing measured
construction charges to eliminate a pass is a possible later optimization only
after proving that it covers that pass's operands, multiplicities and backing.
No cycle improvement is claimed for this design.

## What the source establishes

The following are source findings, not an observed production budget failure:

- [Commit preflight](../../../crates/icydb-core/src/db/executor/mutation/commit_window.rs)
  builds a complete final-row overlay and stages index effects after each row.
  It currently has a relation budget, but no required operation-wide scalar
  construction budget. The final instruction watermark does not supply one.
- [Recovery's reader](../../../crates/icydb-core/src/db/runtime_entity_catalog.rs)
  exposes complete final-row overrides for uniqueness proofs, but reads canonical
  indexes without staging the current batch's prepared index effects.
- [Index planning](../../../crates/icydb-core/src/db/index/plan/mod.rs) derives
  after-image keys before the unchanged-input shortcut. This repairs the omitted
  after-image case; it does not equalize the readers or all performed work.
- [Unique validation](../../../crates/icydb-core/src/db/index/plan/unique.rs)
  may reconstruct a conflicting row's final key to prove that the batch releases
  an existing unique membership. Its two-result probe cap does not account for
  that reconstruction's scalar work.

For example, start with A owning unique value `x`. Submit A: `x -> y`, then insert
B with `x`. Ordinary preflight can stage removal of A's `x` entry before preparing
B. Recovery preparation can still see canonical `x -> A`, then reconstruct A's
final `y` key through the complete final-row overlay to prove the release.
Thus recovery can encode `y` again even though normal preflight did not need that
witness. The focused qualification below confirms this reconstruction difference;
it does not establish a resource or instruction bound.

Matching SQL, row counts, encoded byte lengths, or ordinary after-image charges
alone cannot prove matching recovery cost. In particular, final key bytes do not
describe bigint division scratch, predicate evaluation or accepted-value work.

## Budget lifetimes

Use the existing `ConstructionBudget` interface and mutation policy; do not add
a codec-local optional tracker. Required authority must reach queries, ordinary
writes, nested mutation jobs, maintenance and integrity callers together.

| Actual owner | Accounting lifetime | Admission counterpart |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `open_commit_window_structural_inner` | Entire unpublished logical commit, including qualification work | Actual pre-publication work; also charge any enclosing durable-mutation execution without resetting it |
| `replay_recovery_marker` / `publish_marker_bound_journal_batches` | One Replay callback, including all directly applied batches and their simultaneously retained prepared results | Aggregate direct-batch construction, not a fresh allowance per batch |
| `fold_selected_journal_head` | One complete journal batch, startup or online | That batch's transition construction under its predecessor authority |
| `verify_recovered_effects` | Entire marker's final effect set and shared accepted-context cache | Final-after-image construction across the marker, not a fresh allowance per row |

Replay, each Fold callback and Verify are already separate stages. They need
independent budgets with the same policy, not one cumulative budget for an
arbitrarily long startup backlog. Each older journal batch must have its own
original admission evidence in the correctness proof; the latest write must
neither scan nor finance the entire backlog.

Today the mutation policy supplies 128 MiB temporary bytes, 16,000,000 expression
steps and 16,000,000 nested-value steps, with its existing instruction ceiling
and failure headroom. Reuse the named policy, not copied numeric constants.
Changing any ceiling or callback boundary is outside this proposal.

## Pre-publication sequence

All steps are synchronous and precede `begin_commit_window_payload` and any
durable marker, journal append, index/row publication or identity-range advance.

1. Create the ordinary operation's required construction authority before its
   overlay/setup allocations. Perform ordinary validation/preparation and form
   the exact unpublished commit payload through the maintained owners.
2. Qualify transition reconstruction for the payload's actual store/batch
   partition. Call the same row/key preparation owner with existing
   `RecoveryReplay` semantics. Use the logical predecessor index state and a
   complete final-row overlay for that recovery batch, without staging the
   current batch's index changes into the qualification reader.
3. Qualify final marker-owned RowPut reconstruction using the existing
   `DerivedRebuild` preparation semantics: no before-image and the final logical
   row/index view. Share accepted setup across the whole verification unit as
   recovery does. This invokes construction, not the physical effect verifier:
   future durable rows obviously cannot be checked before publication.
4. Finish every qualification budget and the actual enclosing operation budget.
   Publish only if all succeed and the normal authority/generation guards still
   hold. Discard qualification results; only ordinary prepared effects may reach
   Apply. No qualification result becomes a durable receipt or recovery bypass.

The reader requirements are binding, not interchangeable convenience choices:

- A Fold's canonical predecessor after earlier batches drain must correspond to
  the original write's logical predecessor. Prove this under the current fold
  ordering, accepted-schema selection and watermark contract. Today's lagging
  canonical store is not necessarily that future predecessor.
- Direct replay may encounter already-applied effects. Prove the covered
  interrupted states and their construction bound; an all-before dry run alone
  does not establish an arbitrary-prefix bound.
- Final verification needs the complete final view, not the pre-write database
  or an intermediate row-order view. Keep candidate unique-index authority and
  the existing mode-specific relation behavior explicit.
- Public structural and typed mutation batches reject cross-store items before
  execution. Lower-level commit payload construction can still group records by
  store into multiple marker batches. Do not widen those public APIs, or use
  their single-store rule as proof for every lower-level marker producer.

Reuse existing read abstractions. If satisfying these requirements needs another
persisted certificate, new recovery route, general simulation engine or global
state search, stop and rescope rather than hiding it in a reader adapter.

## What counts, and what admission promises

Every real qualification allocation/visit also belongs to actual write work.
A phase-local qualification budget bounds that future unit; it is not a refund
or permission to evade the enclosing write/job budget. Counters must remain
sticky after failure and use checked/saturating failure-safe arithmetic.

Construction must charge before allocation and within input-dependent loops:
scalar output growth/copies, escaping, bigint limbs/chunks/divisions, accepted
value conversion, and the backing/visits retained by the qualified unit. Include
recovery-only row-op lists, context caches and prepared-result retention; do not
declare a complete callback bounded from scalar bytes alone. Share each charge's
owner with the real construction operation, not an independent recursive sizer.

For every newly bounded deterministic resource `r`, the implementation must
establish, for every covered internally produced recovery state `s` and unit `u`:

`actual_recovery(u, s, r) <= qualified_bound(u, r) <= existing_limit(r)`

Passing one dry run establishes only its observed construction. It is not the
left-hand inequality. Prove missing-state coverage structurally and with boundary
tests; do not silently supply an arbitrary multiplier. Where a qualification
pass cannot cover future work directly, identify the exact shared owner and
derive its conservative bound before enabling the corresponding recovery limit.
If that cannot be established, the combined encoder/admission landing is not ready.

Instruction units are different. Preflight's instruction reading is neither a
reservation of future IC cycles nor a universal replay upper bound: cache state,
stable I/O and callback setup differ. Retain actual watermarks/failure reserves
and require matched IC qualification for accepted maximum-cost shapes. Do not
substitute native elapsed time, node counts or copied bytes for instructions.
This does not strengthen the current [durability guarantee](../../contracts/DURABILITY.md)
into an unconditional promise against corruption, platform limits or arbitrary
future Wasm/schema changes. Requalify changes to encoding, charging or recovery
behavior; no pre-1.0 compatibility decoder or old-policy fallback is proposed.

## Failure and scope boundaries

- Semantic errors retain their existing type. Construction exhaustion retains
  the original typed resource failure; optional index compilation cannot turn
  it into unsupported encoding or a cached absent prefilter. The unique-key
  reconstruction error mapper must also preserve it.
- Admission failure publishes none of this commit, consumes no durable identity
  range, preserves previous committed work and returns no partial success.
- Recovery remains replay, not fresh application validation. Keep effect
  verification, readiness gating, idempotence and marker clearing unchanged.
  No retry loop, automatic batch splitting or larger replay allowance is added.
- Schema-only publishers, index staging and integrity pages retain their
  separate owners. Inventory all shared-encoder callers; ordinary row preflight
  must not be presented as admitting every marker family or non-row operation.

Need: avoid new deterministic limits making admitted writes unrecoverable.
Simplest insufficient alternative: charge only ordinary preparation, or reset
the same allowance per reconstructed key. Canonical owners: commit admission,
the existing row/key constructor and current recovery stages. Proposed state
delta: bounded, volatile qualification trackers and temporary reader/context
lifetimes during preflight, all discarded before return. No public configuration,
encoder policy, persisted field, callback stage or exported API is added.
Repeated preparation is intentional admission work, not duplicate semantics;
its ongoing code and instruction cost must be reported explicitly.

## Implementation and qualification gate

Keep the existing combined R5 encoder/admission landing. This design is not a
reason to start additional clone-cleanup prerequisites. Before handing it off:

1. Bind the reader/state proof above to source owners, including unique-value
   release, direct partial publication, multi-store partitioning and candidate
   generations. If a proposed pass does not cover them, resolve that gap first.
2. Propagate required budgets and typed failures through all codec consumers;
   implement pre-publication qualification and actual recovery charging together.
3. Test exact-limit/one-over-limit rejection before publication, cumulative
   multi-row/multi-batch accounting, failed finish, nested job charging and
   unchanged current encoder golden bytes. Use test-only reduced limits.
4. Exercise insert/update/delete, unchanged-index updates, unique-value handoff,
   partial/expression and candidate indexes, bigint/escaped-text operands, all
   maintained interruption boundaries, stage loss/restart and online folding.
   Combine cases only where they test a distinct interaction; no broad new harness.
5. Compare permitted instruction/cycle and raw-Wasm measurements on matched
   Rust/dependency/actor builds. Separate normal writes, qualification overhead,
   direct replay, Fold and Verify; include cold setup and late rejection.
   Report peak retained backing separately from cumulative allocation charges.

## Reader-state qualification — 2026-09-10

Six maintained [unique-handoff regressions](../../../crates/icydb-core/src/db/session/write/identity_pre_key_tests/replay_construction_tests/reader_state_tests.rs)
exercise the real write and recovery entrypoints. Each moves row A to a free
unique value before inserting row B with A's previous value. They verify both
final rows, unique enforcement and identity allocation after a rejected duplicate.
Pending startup recovery remains gated; losing volatile stage ownership must not
duplicate the identity advance or lose the handoff.

A temporary test-only counter at
`index/plan/unique.rs::build_unique_index_key_from_row_slots` captured these
successful-path observations. It was removed after collection; the maintained
tests protect semantics, not an exact internal call count.

| Starting state / route | Ordinary write witness rebuilds | Recovery witness rebuilds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct store, marker persisted before row publication | 0 | Replay: 1; Verify: 0 |
| Direct store, first row published, then stage lost after replay | 0 | Initial Replay: 0; repeated Replay: 0; Verify: 0 |
| Direct store, both rows already published | 0 | Replay: 0; Verify: 0 |
| Journaled store, ordinary online convergence | 0 | Fold: 1 |
| Journaled store, first live row published before startup reset | 0 | Replay: 0; Fold: 1; Verify: 0 |
| Journaled store, earlier update still unfolded; stage lost after both folds | 0 | Earlier Fold: 0; handoff Fold: 1; restarted Replay and Verify: 0 |

These are witness reconstruction counts, not all scalar encoder calls, copied
bytes, IC instructions or cycles. The direct all-before pass covers this fixture's
witness work; do not generalize that observation to all construction resources.

The unfolded-predecessor case first commits A: `10 -> 20` without folding it,
then commits A: `20 -> 30` plus B: `20`. Startup folds the earlier transition
before the handoff and preserves both watermarks across a stage restart. This
qualifies why preflight cannot simply use today's lagging canonical row/index
view as the future Fold predecessor. It is a two-batch behavior proof, not a
proof for arbitrary schema transitions or every marker producer.

### Additional obligation: already-applied inserts have a before-image

The all-before qualification is not yet a bound for complete replay construction.
The source establishes a separate difference even when witness work decreases:

1. `prepare_recovered_row_transitions` reads canonical storage for `before` and
   takes `after` from the journal record.
2. An unpublished insert has no before-image. Once that row is directly applied,
   replay constructs the same insert with both before and after present.
3. Previously, `prepare_row_commit_structural_inputs` copied both present images
   and `decode_commit_marker_rows_for_preflight` built two structural readers,
   before the index planner's unchanged-input shortcut. The shared-image repair
   below removes this duplicate construction at those owners.

Consequently, an all-before pass's absent old row does not cover that already-
applied row's complete before-image acquisition and preparation work. This is a
source-derived construction obligation, not an observed exhaustion; scalar
witness counts above do not measure these row buffers. Mixed direct replay may
contain both row states at once. Final verification's `before = None` pass also
does not supply the missing per-Replay-unit bound merely by succeeding separately.

Admission must include the per-row maximum of the admitted predecessor and
already-applied after-image reconstruction, with the complete unit's shared
backing/context costs. Compute any required extra allowance at the actual shared
copy/decode owners or through their bounded construction, not from encoded key
lengths, an arbitrary multiplier, or a per-row budget reset. Matching witness
rebuilds is only one part of that proof. No new simulation or persisted state is
authorized by this finding.

### Implemented boundary: shared identical images

Commit preparation now uses one private shared/distinct representation for raw
rows and validated readers. Exact before/after byte identity shares one copy
and one reader under the same borrowed accepted row contract. Both semantic
sides remain present; index, candidate, relation and finalization consumers
continue through their existing flow. Different images retain separate full
validation, and malformed rows or primary-key mismatches reject the complete
pair. The existing raw-row size cap is checked before equality or copying.

| Images at shared commit preparation | Raw-row copies | Validated readers |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Absent before, present after | 1 | 1 |
| Byte-identical before and after | 1 | 1 |
| Different before and after | 2 | 2 |
| Both absent (allowed replay case) | 0 | 0 |

These are owner-local construction counts, not IC cost measurements or complete
callback bounds. In particular, `prepare_recovered_row_transitions` still
acquires canonical before-images and journal after-images into `CommitRowOp`,
and its canonical reader now borrows those operations (see acquisition below).
The operation buffers remain part of admission. Equality adds a size-bounded
linear walk when two images are present and must be covered by future
construction charging. Sharing validation
does not remove all old/new projection work or after-image key derivation.

Four new direct tests qualify shared construction and both semantic views,
distinct/absent sides, malformed images, key mismatch and oversized rejection.
Existing recovery and after-image admission tests qualify the unchanged
downstream behavior. No recovery budget, persisted certificate, stage or
instruction/size guarantee is added by this repair.

### Implemented boundary: recovery image acquisition ownership

`CanonicalCommitReader` now borrows final keys and byte slices from the existing
immutable operation list. Its map retains no second row/key payload copy. A
present map entry with no row still means deletion, not canonical fallback.
The constructor checks the existing raw-row size limit and rejects duplicate
keys, including deletions. Requested witnesses are still copied into owned
`RawRow` results through the maintained reader contract; they cannot mutate the
operation's images. `prepare_recovered_row_transitions` moves each canonical
read's owned buffer into `CommitRowOp::before`, removing the temporary second
before-image allocation without changing the storage read.

The remaining allocation inventory for one recovery batch is:

| Owner | Payload/backing still constructed |
| --- | --- |
| Decoded journal batch | Recorded after-images and record/key/path backing; retained by the caller |
| Transition acquisition | One copied after-image per RowPut; one owned canonical before-image read per present predecessor; operation and ordinal vectors; operation key/path backing |
| Final-row lookup | One reference-map entry per row operation, including deletion overrides; B-tree backing/comparisons remain |
| Witness lookup | One owned row per actual successful lookup, possibly repeated for the same row; lookup key construction and canonical fallback work remain |
| Commit preparation | Shared/distinct raw copies and readers described above, equality walks, accepted contexts, projections and prepared effects |
| Record results | A record-sized result vector plus prepared results retained by the real Replay/Fold owner |

Let `A` be the sum of recorded after-image lengths and `B` the sum of canonical
before-image lengths actually read for that batch. Transition operations retain
`A + B` image bytes (excluding allocation slack). The final-row map now adds
zero image payload bytes instead of another `A`; moving the before-row buffer
also avoids copying `B` cumulatively during acquisition. This is source-derived
ownership accounting, not a measured peak-heap or instruction reduction.

The current 16-MiB journal envelope bounds `A` plus its encoded record metadata.
It does not bound `B`: deletes record no after-image, and updates can replace a
large predecessor with a small row. The 16,384-record cap and 4-MiB admitted row
cap are separate bounds, not proof of fitting the 128-MiB mutation policy.
For internally produced direct-replay row-prefix states, each before-image may
be the admitted predecessor or the final image; acquisition must cover that
per-row maximum, including an originally absent insert's now-present final row.
This observation still depends on the covered authority/state contract; it is
not a bound for arbitrary corruption or future schema changes. A size check
after a stable-store read also does not bound that read's earlier allocation.

Required budget propagation must charge actual remaining copies, map/vector
backing and repeated witness work, with conservative pre-publication coverage
for those allowed predecessor states. Do not enable a new recovery rejection
gate from the journal size alone or from this reduced retention inventory.
Candidate/schema authority and complete callback retention remain open.

### Mixed-state image bound — 2026-09-10

This is an owner-local payload/reader bound, not implemented accounting or a
complete replay bound. Let `b_i` be a row's actual persisted predecessor bytes
and `a_i` its recorded final bytes; absence has length zero. For the covered
fixed-authority, canonical-final-image case, an interrupted row has before-image
`p_i` equal to either `b_i` or `a_i`. Arbitrary combinations are a conservative
superset of complete-row publication prefixes. Define:

- `A = sum(len(a_i))`, the copied recorded after-image payload.
- `M = sum(max(len(b_i), len(a_i)))`, the maximum acquired before-image payload.
- `D(b, a)`, the payload bytes actually copied by `CommitRowImages`: one copy
  for identical present images, otherwise the sum of present images.

The transition operation list's image payload is at most `A + M`. The weaker
`A + max(sum(len(b_i)), A)` is insufficient: a prefix can contain large final
images from growing rows and large predecessors from later shrinking/deleted
rows simultaneously. These quantities exclude capacities, vector/map backing,
keys, paths, decoded journal retention and temporary storage-read allocations.

At shared preparation, `D(a_i, a_i) <= D(b_i, a_i)` for every transition:

| Transition | Original image copies / readers | Already-applied image copies / readers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Insert | Final image / one | Shared final image / one |
| Distinct update | Predecessor plus final / two | Shared final image / one |
| Delete | Predecessor / one | None / zero |
| Byte-unchanged update | Shared image / one | Shared image / one |

The original transition therefore bounds raw payload copying and the number of
validated reader constructions at this owner for every mixture. Reader counts
alone do not bound decoding steps or retained nested values: those charges must
follow the same actual immutable image/contract construction. Equality work is
separate: an unapplied insert performs no two-image comparison, whereas its
already-applied form compares the final image with itself. At most `len(a_i)`
byte positions can be compared per row; the existing size checks precede that
walk. This is a prospective byte-visit operand, not an instruction measurement
or an enabled charge.

The [image regression](../../../crates/icydb-core/src/db/commit/prepare/tests/images.rs)
uses the real raw-image and validated-reader constructors for all 32 old/final
combinations across these five fixtures. It checks each row's copy/reader bound
and demonstrates why the whole-batch endpoint maximum undercounts acquisition.
It does not simulate storage, candidate indexes, relation projection or unique
witnesses. Existing recovery regressions retain responsibility for those routes.

Binding this result into admission still requires the correct operands.
`session/write.rs` canonicalizes a loaded predecessor into `canonical_before`
before forming `CommitRowOp::before`; recovery reads physical canonical storage.
Those representations must not be assumed byte-identical across accepted
append-only layouts. The qualifier must cover the actual future persisted
predecessor, not blindly count its normalized operation image. Likewise, the
two-state reduction requires the recorded final image to equal the applied
canonical image under the selected authority. Keep historical-layout/schema
equivalence and all marker producers in the remaining proof; these fixtures do
not establish either globally. No extra recovery pass, estimator or limit is
introduced by this result.

### Pending-candidate authority qualification

The current authority chain is explicit in source:

| Construction route | Accepted selection and candidate handling |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary write | `AcceptedRuntimeEntity::prepare_commit_context` selects current live catalog; the shared constructor consumes that selection and pending unique/candidate relation projections participate |
| Journal replay / Fold | The runtime-entity caller selects canonical catalog for journal-backed stores; the shared constructor derives candidate owners from that supplied snapshot |
| Direct replay | `replay_recovery_marker` restores/applies live-schema checkpoints before row preparation; the non-journal path then selects that accepted live catalog |
| Final Verify | An operation-local `DerivedRebuild` context selects current accepted authority after tail drain; unique-candidate preparation still derives its after-image key, while candidate relation effects are excluded |

Accepted-schema integrity requires exactly one matching unique candidate and
matching activation epoch/physical generation. `UniqueConstraintProjection`
uses that accepted physical owner. The activation runner also validates its
job's candidate generation. No additional generation authority is needed at
commit preparation. `CommitPrepareContextCache` remains local to a synchronous
operation and does not authorize reuse across schema mutations or callbacks.

Three [candidate-authority regressions](../../../crates/icydb-core/src/db/session/write/identity_pre_key_tests/replay_construction_tests/candidate_authority_tests.rs)
qualify the maintained pending-candidate case:

- Delete preparation sees a live candidate's exact generation while canonical
  preparation still sees the predecessor; after folding, a fresh recovery
  context produces the same index-owner effects as the live context.
- A delete interrupted at marker persistence behind an unfolded activation
  completes through real Replay/Fold/Verify, including loss of volatile stage
  ownership. The final canonical catalog retains the candidate generation and
  matching activation epoch. Schema publication and row records are separate
  journal batches; global journal-head ordering folds the earlier schema first.
- Direct replay restores a lost volatile accepted catalog from its durable
  checkpoint before preparing an identity-bound mixed batch. A delete from the
  candidate-owned entity accompanies an identity insert into another entity in
  the same store. The restored complete bundle matches the original; row/index
  effects complete and readiness waits for verification.

These tests use an enforcing-new-writes candidate, not a populated validation
generation or completed promotion. They do not prove every promotion/abort,
validation-job, relation-candidate or multi-store marker interaction. The direct
test loses catalog/stage state in the native fixture, not the entire IC heap.
Heap-only deletes do not enter durable row recovery payloads: the existing
commit-payload owner includes heap row records only when an identity advance
binds that store. The first fixture incorrectly expected an isolated heap delete
to replay; it was corrected to the supported identity-bound case. This does not
give heap stores a general durability guarantee or widen public cross-store
batch admission.

#### Implemented selection handoff for pre-publication qualification

Calling the runtime-entity helper with `RecoveryReplay` during a normal write
is insufficient: it selects today's canonical snapshot for a journaled store,
which can predate the live candidate. The future Fold occurs
after earlier accepted schema batches have drained. A dry run against the
lagging snapshot can omit candidate-key construction altogether.

The single `prepare_commit_context_from_catalog_selection` constructor now
requires an already-resolved accepted selection independently of preparation
mode. Entity/path/store identity derives from that selection; existing verified
accepted-row decoding owns its interpretation. The recorded operation
fingerprint remains separate, preserving compatible historical replay rather
than requiring it to equal the current selection's fingerprint.

The existing runtime-entity caller owns live/canonical selection with unchanged
rules. Actual Replay/Fold still selects canonical authority, and direct replay
uses the restored checkpoint. Write-time qualification can supply the write's
logical predecessor with recovery semantics without another selector enum,
generated snapshot or second preparation implementation. Operation-local cache
lifetimes are unchanged; no qualification pass or new recovery budget is enabled.

The candidate-selection regression now also supplies live authority explicitly
with replay semantics before folding and verifies the candidate generation is
retained. An additional regression preserves typed wrong-entity and mismatched
recorded-fingerprint rejection at row preparation.

### Proof coverage and remaining gates

- Qualified: maintained unique handoff behavior across the six reader states,
  the observed write/replay witness difference, an unfolded predecessor and
  stage restart. Existing public same-store admission narrows the public-batch
  partition obligation; low-level payload grouping remains broader. Identical
  images now share raw-row and reader construction at commit preparation. The
  final-row map borrows operation backing, and canonical before-row acquisition
  transfers its owned buffer; remaining allocation operands are inventoried above.
  Pending unique-candidate live/canonical selection and exact direct checkpoint
  restoration are covered by the original three authority tests. The extended
  explicit-selection coverage and fourth test qualify the constructor handoff.
- Qualified at the image owner: the per-row maximum bounds acquired payload,
  while original canonical transitions bound shared raw-copy payload and reader
  counts across all old/final mixtures. Equality has a separate byte-visit bound.
- Open: physical/normalized predecessor and final-image equivalence, complete
  decoder/backing costs for covered direct prefixes; full candidate lifecycle
  and schema-transition equivalence; all actual
  lower-level marker producers; aggregate retained backing and complete-unit
  charge coverage. Passing these six cases does not discharge those obligations.
- Next: use the explicit selection handoff while retaining actual recovery's
  canonical/checkpoint authority. Complete conservative bounds
  for the inventoried acquisition/equality work and remaining lifecycle coverage
  with the combined required-budget/encoder/admission outcome. Do not ship an all-before-only gate
  as recovery-safe, add a larger recovery profile or start unrelated cleanup.

No new admission guarantee, instruction bound or size delta has been measured.
Combined recovery-admission implementation remains gated by these proof and
qualification obligations, not another approval of the same policy direction.
