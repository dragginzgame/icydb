## Promotion Evidence

Toko's durable mutation-job adoption audit established three immediate
blockers:

- a frozen start timestamp can make a later matching managed-timestamp row
  permanently unexecutable;
- row-count-only Forward admission can scan roughly 832 MiB of maximum-size
  nonmatching rows or repeatedly select a matching prefix that exceeds
  structural staging bytes; and
- an unadvanced Active job has no safe cancellation path and permanently
  retains shared capacity.

The promotion-time source review also confirmed:

- Verify observes store-wide rather than entity-specific mutation revision;
- separate multi-job starts have no rollback, making sequence-zero cancellation
  the smallest coordinator recovery primitive;
- target errors and lifecycle metrics do not preserve the operational reason;
  and
- current documentation still says 224/56 and retains the removed
  caller-custodied mutation continuation description.

Commit-marker atomicity, exact successful-request replay, bounded count-based
happy-path steps, stable Verify safety, and upgrade recovery remain accepted
foundations. The promoted gap is safe failure without guaranteed progress.

## Current Decisions

- Patches 1 through 5 satisfy the mutation-job liveness prerequisites for Toko
  adoption, including cross-message, wide-row, sustained multi-entity,
  operational diagnostics, capacity, and terminal recovery proofs.
- Statement time remains frozen; writer-managed time becomes per advance.
- Forward gets independent raw scan-byte and exact writer-owned staged-byte
  budgets; Verify keeps a separate raw scan-byte budget.
- The final throughput policy admits 4,096 Forward keys and a writer-derived
  240-row maximum while raising Verify to 4,096 keys and retaining both 16 MiB byte
  gates.
- An anomalous managed-time regression or one candidate that cannot fit the
  current policy becomes a bounded terminal `RestartRequired` reason rather
  than an unchanged Active retry loop.
- An admitted deterministic execution-budget failure also becomes terminal
  `RestartRequired(ExecutionBudgetPolicyExceeded)`; it cannot leave the same
  checkpoint Active.
- Cancellation is limited to the exact initial sequence-zero state, and every
  logical job incarnation requires a fresh job ID.
- Inventory validates all retained slots and returns typed
  `CorruptProgressStore` rather than partial capacity on corruption.
- With the global cap at 64 and reservation at eight, non-integrity insertion
  rejects at pre-insert count 56 or greater; integrity insertion may reach 64.
- Generic store-wide revision consumers remain unchanged; only mutation Verify
  uses entity revision.
- The current-convergence gate initializes every accepted predecessor entity
  tag to the validated store-wide revision; afterward missing metadata fails
  closed with no fallback.
- Forward-to-Verify stores the journal-prepared exact resulting target
  revision, and every Verify page compares that one retained baseline before
  and after its scan.
- Forward preparation has one measured fixed transient peak and does not clone
  a second page-sized candidate buffer.
- Verify restart cause is reported through bounded metrics rather than changing
  the persisted replay-receipt shape solely for observability.
- Atomic multi-job start, partial abandonment, rollback, ownership, and quotas
  remain out of scope.

## Implementation Status

Patch 1 is implemented as the `0.233.0` worktree handoff:

- retained operation time remains canonical statement time;
- Forward captures one current managed-write timestamp after authority and
  runtime validation and passes it to the structural writer for the whole page;
- the structural writer owns the shared monotonicity predicate;
- anomalous managed-time regression commits terminal
  `RestartRequired(ManagedTimestampRegression)` progress before row staging;
- the timestamp-policy component changes the batch-policy identity; and
- test-admin audit endpoints drive one advance per PocketIC message without
  changing the production export set.

No new job state, progress allocation, timestamp field, compatibility path, or
second mutation executor was added.

Patch 2 is implemented in the same `0.233.0` worktree handoff:

- Forward and Verify each enforce an independent 16 MiB raw-key-plus-row scan
  budget before full decode while retaining their 208-key maxima;
- Forward retains its 56-row maximum and packs against the structural writer's
  exact 16 MiB key/canonical-before/materialized-after staging charge;
- a pull-based scanner presents one candidate at a time, advances a pending
  candidate checkpoint only after writer admission, and never reads beyond the
  first staging-rejected candidate;
- the next advance revisits that excluded candidate, while scan-admitted
  nonmatching rows advance normally;
- one individually impossible candidate terminalizes as
  `RestartRequired(CandidateExceedsBatchPolicy)` without target-row effects;
- the final Forward/Verify count and byte limits and checkpoint rules all bind
  the current batch-policy identity; and
- progress-only pages and row-mutation pages continue through the existing
  progress replacement and structural commit authorities.

The current maximum valid raw key plus row is 4,194,566 bytes, and the maximum
valid exact key/before/after charge is 8,388,870 bytes, so both 16 MiB policies
admit one maximum valid row. The fixed row-buffer portion of the transient peak
is at most 25,166,086 bytes plus bounded writer metadata; no second page-sized
candidate vector is retained.

Patch 3 is implemented as the `0.233.1` worktree handoff:

- the trusted session and public Rust facade expose idempotent
  `cancel_unadvanced_mutation_job`, limited to a decoded current-format initial
  Forward continuation and the exact sequence-zero/no-effects state;
- cancellation validates and removes under one shared progress-store borrow,
  returns success when absent, rejects every nonzero retained sequence, and
  deliberately accepts a prior batch-policy identity without executing it;
- the application contract makes every mutation-job ID a single-incarnation
  identity, so a later logical job must use a fresh ID and no cancellation
  tombstone or reusable-ID ABA state is added;
- one trusted `progress_job_inventory` validates every retained key, envelope,
  family payload, and state closure before returning capacity, family counts,
  IDs, bounded lifecycle, and sequence facts; one bad slot fails the entire
  operation with `CorruptProgressStore`;
- mutation and generic resumable insertion now reject when the pre-insert
  retained count is 56 or greater, while integrity work may consume the exact
  eight reserved slots up to the unchanged hard limit of 64; and
- existing retained records and replay lookup remain valid at or above the
  non-integrity threshold, with no persisted quota, owner identity, format
  version, progress allocation, or public canister endpoint added.

Patch 4 is implemented as the `0.233.2` worktree handoff:

- `JournalTailStore` owns one current version-1 entity-revision control marker
  plus sorted packed revision chunks bounded by the accepted 4,096-entity
  schema limit;
- current convergence initializes a missing authority from the validated
  store-wide data revision without scanning rows, while a present incomplete,
  malformed, or accepted-tag-mismatched authority fails closed;
- direct initial schema publication and journaled schema publication both
  publish the exact accepted entity set, preserving retained revisions and
  conservatively initializing newly accepted tags;
- each row batch advances only its distinct affected entity tags to the exact
  journal-owned post-batch revision, including idempotent marker replay, while
  folding leaves the authority intact;
- Forward obtains the mutating page's exact resulting target revision from
  journal preparation, and zero-update Forward obtains the current target
  revision from the same owner; and
- every Verify message compares the same retained target baseline before and
  after its scan, retains it across intermediate checkpoints, ignores writes
  to other entities in the same store, and restarts on a target-entity write.

Patch 5 is implemented as the `0.233.3` worktree handoff:

- public target mutation failures carry only
  `StagingByteBudgetExceeded` or `Other`, preserving an actionable bounded
  reason without leaking target data;
- a deterministic execution-budget divergence for policy-admitted work commits
  terminal `RestartRequired(ExecutionBudgetPolicyExceeded)` progress before
  returning, so it cannot leave the same Active checkpoint retryable forever;
- every non-replay advance executes inside one fixed 30-billion-instruction
  allocation with a 5-billion failure reserve and explicit watermarks before
  commit-marker publication; exact replay returns before installing it;
- Forward now examines at most 4,096 keys and stages at most 240 updates, where
  240 is derived from canonical commit-work and maximum-index-fanout authority;
  Verify remains independently bounded at 4,096 keys;
- the complete execution-budget profile contributes its owner-derived identity
  to the final batch-policy identity;
- retained immutable entity source paths resolve through source-key authority
  during advance rather than the editable SQL-name lookup;
- successful mutation-job start, state load, replay, Forward/Verify commit,
  transition, Verify restart, completion, terminal restart, cancellation,
  acknowledgement, and inventory operations emit bounded lifecycle metrics;
- committed-step metrics retain exact key, row, scan-byte, and staged-byte
  totals and maxima, while capacity metrics retain exact validated record bytes;
- metrics contain no job IDs, SQL, keys, values, replay keys, or continuation
  bytes, and the extended audit query remains test-admin-only;
- current facade, SQL, and persisted-format documentation now agrees with the
  implemented ownership, timing, byte, revision, cancellation, and capacity
  contracts; and
- the final batch-policy identity is `0x195dc27a`, including the explicit
  execution-budget terminalization rule and every preceding 0.233 policy input.

The semantic policy input revisions are policy components rather than format
versions; every pre-1.0 persisted/runtime representation remains exactly the
sole current version-1 form. A retained mutation job created by an earlier
0.233 landing follows the existing terminal `BatchPolicyChanged` restart
contract rather than executing under mixed semantics.
